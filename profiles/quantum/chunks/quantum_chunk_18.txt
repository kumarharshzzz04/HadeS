In quantum computing, we use two types of matrices to apply our operations. One of them is unitary matrices. These have the property that given a unitary matrix u, u times u dagger is equal to the identity matrix, so u dagger is its inverse. Geometrically, when a unitary matrix acts on a vector, the length of the vector stays the same, but it is rotated or flipped. This property may not seem very useful, but once we start talking about probabilities in quantum computers, it will all start to make sense.
