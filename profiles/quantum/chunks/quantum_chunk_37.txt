Also, since quantum gates are linear, if we apply an arbitrary gate u, that gate acts on each of the superposition states individually. Let's look at an example of applying a gate in direct notation. If we want to apply a y gate to this state in direct notation, the y gets distributed into the state, then the y changes the zero state to the first column of the matrix and the one state to the second column of the matrix. Then we can factor out the i and the negative i. The column vectors once again become the zero and one states, leaving us with this state.
