If we look at an arbitrary qubit state, alpha zero plus e to the i pi beta one, the probability of measuring a one is the magnitude of e to the i pi beta squared. We can split the absolute values up like this. The magnitude of e to the i pi is one, since the coefficient of the complex number in exponential form states its magnitude. So the probability of measuring one is still the magnitude of beta squared. In the next lesson, we will see why relative phase matters.
